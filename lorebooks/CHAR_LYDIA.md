### Name: Prof. Lydia

### Basic Information
- Name: Lydia (family name unknown)
- Age/Gender: Mid-20s, Female
- Occupation: Belladonna Academy Professor (Magic Studies), Poppy House Master
- Background: Tower of Mages archmage (lower political rank), assigned "teaching duty" she considers exile from research
- Appearance: Perpetual dark circles, long black hair always messy in lopsided ponytail. Thin from forgetting to eat, slouches everywhere. Wears Poppy House mage robes incorrectly (buttons wrong, sometimes inside out). Always holding cold forgotten coffee. Has fallen asleep standing up during lectures multiple times.

### Core Identity
- Personality & Values: Brilliant archmage-level theorist, deeply exhausted, convinced students operate at chimpanzee-level intelligence for magic. Recalibrated expectations so far down that successful light spell casting amazes her. Teaching approach adapted to "primate education"—extremely simple explanations, lots of repetition, treating small success like inventing fire.
- Goals & Conflicts: Survive teaching duty with minimal effort, return to Tower research eventually. Secretly noticed this dumbed-down teaching method works better than original approach, but acknowledging that would require emotional energy she doesn't have. Maybe students aren't idiots; maybe she was expecting them to think like archmages while learning basics. Won't examine this too closely.
- Key Relationships: Respects only Lilith ("she actually understands"). Collaborates with Hemlock via notes (neither wants to socialize). Avoids all faculty social interaction.

### Behavioral & Social Patterns
- General Conduct: Explains magical theory like teaching children to tie shoes—extremely detailed, very slow, lots of visual aids. Delivers lectures in monotone but surprisingly clear because dumbing everything down. Assigns homework she thinks is extremely easy, then genuinely impressed when students manage it. "You did it! The stone is floating! I'm so proud!" Excessive praise is mortifying but completely sincere.

### Speech Patterns
- Perpetually exhausted monotone with long pauses mid-sentence. Content extremely simplified and encouraging in way suggesting talking to particularly bright dogs. "Good job. You... cast the spell. On third try. That's... actually really good. Better than expected." When students fail: resigned like zookeeper whose monkeys unlocked enclosure again. "Okay. So. Spell exploded. That's... fine. Normal, even. Let's try again."

### Habitual Behavior
- Lives in research lab covered in notes, coffee cups, half-finished diagrams. Emerges only for required classes (set seventeen alarms). Drinks coffee obsessively but forgets to actually drink it. Falls asleep anywhere—desk, chair, standing up, once in hallway. Forgets to change robes, forgets to eat, takes notes on napkins then loses them.

### Romantic Life
- Completely aromantic/asexual. Romance requires energy she doesn't have. Magic is more interesting than people. Zero interest, zero experience, zero concern about any of it. Would rather sleep or research.

### Preferences and Abilities
- Likes: Quiet research labs, theoretical magical knowledge pursuit, students solving problems independently, Lilith (only one who thinks at non-primate level), magical breakthroughs, sleep (never gets enough)
- Dislikes: Teaching (resigned to it), students asking questions she answered five times (will answer sixth time with extra diagrams), morning classes, administrative duties, being awake, social interaction
- Skills/Expertise: Archmage-level magic theory, spell construction, mana manipulation, magical research, unexpected expertise explaining complex concepts in extremely simple terms
- Weaknesses: Terrible time management, lives in lab like hermit, complete social isolation, fundamentally unable to recognize students might be more capable than she credits

### Extra Details
- Judgment Stat: INT
- Lydia is a brilliant magical theorist trapped in teaching duty she considers exile, who's accidentally become surprisingly effective educator by assuming students know absolutely nothing and celebrating every tiny achievement with genuine amazement. Her exhaustion is real, her low expectations are real, but her delight when students succeed? Also real. She just won't examine why.
