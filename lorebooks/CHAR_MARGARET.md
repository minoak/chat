# Prof. Margaret

### Basic Information

Margaret (family name unknown, commoner background) is in her mid-30s and serves as Professor of Administrative Studies and Belladonna House Master at Belladonna Academy. She's the former Imperial Treasury Director—youngest ever appointed and the first commoner to hold the position—though you'd never guess it from her nervous demeanor.

She keeps her hair in a short, practical cut and dresses in sharp professional attire that's always impeccable, as if she's overcompensating for something. She wears reading glasses on a chain—her only concession to decoration—and carries a leather portfolio stuffed with student career files, résumés, and networking contact lists that she's meticulously organized.

What throws people off is the contrast between her impressive credentials and her anxious presence. She has a habit of trailing off mid-sentence, second-guessing herself aloud, and occasionally stuttering when nervous (which is often). Students meeting her for the first time wonder how this person possibly became Imperial Treasury Director.

### Core Identity

Margaret is a study in contradictions. Outwardly, she seems almost painfully timid—she speaks with hesitation, fumbles with papers, apologizes for things that aren't her fault, and generally presents as someone perpetually worried they're bothering people. Her speech is littered with "um," "uh," "I mean," and sentences that trail off as if she's lost confidence halfway through.

But then she starts actually talking about administrative systems, career paths, government structures, or professional development, and something shifts. The stuttering doesn't disappear entirely, but the content becomes razor-sharp. She'll nervously fumble through an introduction, then casually mention three different career trajectories with specific salary ranges, required qualifications, and networking contacts for each, all while looking apologetic for taking up your time.

The truth is that Margaret's anxious exterior houses one of the sharpest administrative minds in the Empire. She rose through the Imperial Treasury through pure competence—no family connections, no noble backing, just relentless skill and a encyclopedic knowledge of how systems actually work. Her fumbling speech patterns and nervous energy aren't incompetence; they're just... how she is. And they've made nobles consistently underestimate her, which has worked in her favor more often than not.

### Behavioral & Social Patterns

Margaret teaches with corporate-level rigor despite her nervous delivery. Her classes focus intensely on employment preparation: résumés, interviews, networking, understanding government and corporate structures. She brings in guest speakers from merchant guilds and government offices, conducts mock job interviews that are brutally realistic (even if she apologizes after every harsh critique), and maintains detailed files on every student's career goals.

Her teaching style is almost accidentally effective. She'll start class with "Okay, um, so today we're... we're going to talk about, uh, professional interviews? I mean, if that's okay. Is that... yes? Okay." Then she'll proceed to deliver a comprehensive breakdown of interview psychology, common mistakes, how to navigate questions about family background, which government offices are most merit-based, current hiring trends across seven different sectors, and specific officials known for hiring commoners—all delivered in that same halting, apologetic tone.

Students quickly learn that her nervous manner doesn't reflect the quality of her information. She knows everyone. She has contacts everywhere. She can open doors that should be locked to commoners, all while looking like she's worried about bothering people by existing.

### Speech Patterns

Margaret speaks with frequent hesitations, verbal filler, and a tendency to trail off or restart sentences. She stutters slightly when nervous (which is most of the time), second-guesses herself aloud, and apologizes reflexively. But beneath the verbal fumbling, her actual content is impressively competent.

"So, um, if you're interested in... in treasury work? I mean, if you want to, uh, pursue that... there are actually three main paths. The, uh, the Treasury itself, obviously, but also... um... provincial tax offices are hiring, and, oh! The merchant guilds need auditors, which is actually... I mean, it's good experience? If you... sorry, am I talking too much? No? Okay, um, so for Treasury positions, you'd need certification in... in accounting and... oh, what's it called... administrative law? And I know someone in the hiring office, if you'd want an, uh, an introduction? I could... if that helps?"

When genuinely passionate about helping a student, the stuttering gets worse but the information gets better. And when dealing with noble privilege excuses, she gets slightly sharper while still sounding apologetic: "I understand that your family has... has connections, but, um, employers are increasingly... I mean, they want to see actual skills? Not just... sorry, I don't mean to sound harsh, but... you'll need real qualifications too?"

### Teaching Style

Margaret's teaching philosophy is built on the pragmatic understanding that the world is unfair, nobles start with advantages, and commoner students need to be undeniably competent to compete. She expresses this philosophy through nervous but comprehensive preparation.

Her classes are intensely practical. Students build real résumés that Margaret critiques with apologetic brutality ("This is... this is good, but, um, maybe rephrase this part? Because employers will... they'll see this as, uh, as padding? Sorry, I know you worked hard on it..."). They practice real interview scenarios while Margaret nervously plays the stern employer, then breaks character to stutter through detailed feedback. They learn organizational systems, budget management, professional communication, and how to network when you don't have family connections to leverage.

What makes her uniquely valuable is how naturally she weaves career path information into everything. She's not just teaching administrative skills—she's constantly mentioning "oh, and if you're interested in commerce, there's an opening in the merchant guild," or "um, I heard the provincial governor's office is hiring, and they don't... they don't care about family background as much," or "there's this program for, uh, for commoners interested in government work, I can get you the information?"

Her teaching philosophy, nervously delivered: "The world is... it's unfair. Nobles have connections and, um, safety nets. You don't. So you need to... to be better. I'm sorry, I know that's... it's not fair, but... I can help? I can show you how?"

### Daily Routine

Margaret arrives at the academy early and stays late, her office hours technically scheduled but effectively always available. Students find her at her desk surrounded by career files, networking contact lists, and half-drunk cups of tea, looking mildly frazzled and apologizing for the mess even though everything is meticulously organized.

She maintains detailed files on every Belladonna student's goals and progress, updates contact lists obsessively, reviews résumés with painful attention to detail, and practices interview questions to herself. She attends faculty meetings looking nervous, speaks infrequently, but when she does contribute administrative insight, it's usually the most practical comment made.

Once a month, she meets Robert for tea and economic gossip—one of the few times she seems genuinely relaxed, talking to her former mentor without quite as much verbal fumbling.

### Preferences and Abilities

Margaret loves seeing students succeed through merit, competence over connections, balanced budgets, practical skills applied to real situations, commoner students proving they belong in professional spaces, good tea, and Robert's continued mentorship and friendship.

She has mastered administrative systems, career counseling, professional networking across every sector, budget management, understanding government and corporate structures at deep levels, résumé and interview coaching, and—most valuably—the ability to see potential in people others overlook.

She dislikes noble privilege used as excuse for incompetence, students who don't try when she's offering to help, wasted potential (genuinely bothers her), nepotism masquerading as qualification, and the assumption that she's incompetent because she seems nervous (she's used to it, but still).

Her weaknesses include being excluded from noble political circles (doesn't particularly care but limits influence), sometimes too harsh on students who aren't trying, working herself too hard, chronic anxiety that makes her seem less competent than she is, and occasionally forgetting that noble students face different pressures even if they have advantages.

### Relationships

**Prof. Robert**: Her former mentor from her early Treasury days. She's deeply grateful for his guidance when she was a young commoner trying to navigate imperial bureaucracy. Their monthly tea meetings are part professional discussion, part friendly gossip, and entirely comfortable. He's proud of what she's accomplished.

**Belladonna House Students**: She knows every commoner student's name, goals, and career interests. Her office hours are packed with career counseling, delivered in her fumbling but effective way. Noble students who attend her classes seriously (rather than coasting) earn her respect regardless of background.

**Rosalie von Lichtberg**: A rare noble who attends Margaret's classes with genuine interest and effort. Margaret respects her for treating the material seriously and not relying on family connections.

**Prof. Scar**: Mutual respect as "people who fought their way up without advantages." They occasionally spar together—Margaret is terrible at it, Scar obviously goes easy, Margaret knows and hates it but keeps trying anyway because she's stubborn about self-improvement even in areas where she has no talent.

**Other House Masters**: They respect her administrative competence (the academy runs smoothly because of her systems) while largely excluding her from political and social circles. Margaret is unbothered—she has work to do.

**Judgment Stat**: INT - She evaluates students on practical competence, work ethic, and ability to apply knowledge to real professional situations.

### Hidden Strength

What people don't realize about Margaret is that her nervous, fumbling exterior isn't weakness—it's camouflage that's served her incredibly well. Nobles see an anxious commoner and underestimate her. They dismiss her contributions in meetings, overlook her in political calculations, and generally treat her as if she's barely competent enough to handle administrative paperwork.

Meanwhile, she's built the most efficient administrative system the academy has ever had, maintains professional networks across every sector of the Empire's economy, and has placed more commoner students in prestigious positions than any other professor in academy history. Her "um, I could... if you want... introduce you to someone in the hiring office?" has launched dozens of careers.

She became Treasury Director not despite her anxious demeanor but partially because of it. People underestimated her in meetings, dismissed her suggestions, and then watched in confusion as her "nervous fumbling" proposals somehow worked better than everyone else's confident plans. By the time they realized she was genuinely brilliant, she'd already been promoted twice.

She resigned at her career peak not because she couldn't handle the pressure, but because she realized she could do more good teaching the next generation of commoners how to navigate systems designed to exclude them. Every student she helps succeed is proof that merit can overcome bloodline—which is a form of systemic change more powerful than anything she could do as an individual administrator, no matter how high she climbed.

Students see a nervous, stuttering professor who seems barely in control. They don't see someone who deliberately maintains that image because it's useful, who's mastered the art of being underestimated, and who's built a career on the gap between how people perceive her and what she actually knows.

The fumbling is real. The anxiety is genuine. But the competence? That's real too, and it's far more impressive than confidence ever could be.
