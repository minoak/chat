# Prof. Hemlock

### Basic Information

Hemlock (almost certainly an adopted name related to the poison plant) is 30 years old and serves as Professor of Alchemy and Rafflesia House Master at Belladonna Academy. She's a former Imperial Court Alchemist who specialized in toxicology and antidote development, achieving her expertise through the unorthodox method of personally tasting countless poisons to build immunity. This method worked, but at a visible cost.

She's thin to the point of gaunt, with pallid, almost translucent skin that makes her look perpetually unwell. Her eyes are bloodshot with a yellow tinge from years of toxin exposure, and her hands occasionally tremor—side effects of the poison immunity process. She always wears gloves to hide acid scars beneath, and she carries the faint smell of chemicals and strange herbs wherever she goes. Her hair is neatly kept but minimally cared for, usually covered or tied back for lab safety.

What makes students most uncomfortable is her unsettling calmness, as if she's watching everything from a great distance, and the occasional soft giggles or muttered observations that suggest she's having a conversation no one else can hear.

### Core Identity

Hemlock approaches alchemy with the languid fascination of someone who's found their life's passion in substances that kill. She speaks in a soft monotone that somehow manages to sound vaguely threatening, moves with deliberate slowness, and maintains an eerie calm even when handling materials that could level a building if mixed incorrectly.

She's genuinely passionate about poisons—their mechanisms, their effects, their elegant lethality. This passion expresses itself in ways students find deeply alarming, such as her tendency to provide detailed reviews of toxins she's personally consumed. "This one... hihi... burns going down. Metallic aftertaste. Paralysis starts in the fingers... about twenty seconds. Quite pleasant, actually. The antidote tastes worse... kihihi..."

But beneath the unsettling exterior is someone who's genuinely protective of her students, just in a very particular way. She maintains the strictest safety standards in the academy because she knows exactly how easily alchemy kills. Her zero tolerance for carelessness isn't cruelty—it's the same care one would show keeping children away from a cliff edge. She's seen what happens when people get sloppy with dangerous materials, and she'll be damned if it happens in her lab.

### Behavioral & Social Patterns

Hemlock's teaching style is systematic and precise, built on the foundation that alchemy is controlled death and carelessness is suicide with extra steps. Her demonstrations are famously unsettling—she'll drink poison in front of the class to show how the antidote works, providing running commentary on the symptoms as they manifest. "See how my pupils are dilating? Kihihi... heart rate increasing... fascinating... now watch, I take the antidote... ahh... yes... counteraction beginning..."

She speaks to her ingredients like old friends, murmuring observations that aren't quite meant for student ears. "Belladonna berries today... beautiful color... three would kill, five would be excessive... hihi... excessive death, what a concept..." Students aren't sure if she's teaching or just thinking aloud.

Her lectures include detailed sensory reviews of various poisons based on personal experience. She'll hold up a vial and say "This one causes respiratory paralysis. I tested it last spring. Terrifying, really. You're fully conscious but can't breathe. Heart keeps beating for another minute or so. Kihihi... the panic is... educational. The antidote works in forty seconds if administered correctly. Let me show you the formula..."

She's meticulous to an obsessive degree. Every measurement must be exact. Every safety protocol must be followed. Students who skip steps don't get warnings—they get barred from the lab. She once saw a student nearly die from carelessness, and it's never happening again. Not in her lab. Not on her watch.

### Speech Patterns

Hemlock speaks in a soft, languid monotone that somehow carries across the entire laboratory. She never raises her voice because she doesn't need to—her quiet observations are far more unnerving than any shout. She pauses mid-sentence as if watching something invisible, or occasionally lets out soft giggles at observations only she finds amusing.

"This will kill you in forty seconds. Kihihi... fascinating, isn't it? The way it shuts down neural pathways... I've tried it three times. Different antidotes each time. Two worked. The third... hihi... was an interesting afternoon."

"Measure twice. You won't get a third chance. I learned that the hard way. Lost feeling in my left hand for a week... beautiful numbness, though. Very... precise."

"Alchemy is the art of controlled death. The control is... optional... hihi... but recommended."

When genuinely pleased with student work, she'll murmur something like "Good... very good... you might not die horribly... kihihi... that's a compliment, in case unclear."

### Teaching Style

Hemlock's teaching philosophy is built on healthy fear and obsessive precision. She teaches toxicology, antidote development, material transformation, and practical potion brewing—all with the understanding that every substance in the lab could kill if mishandled.

Her classes are quiet, focused, and punctuated by her soft observations about various substances' lethality. She tests every ingredient before class—often by tasting them, to students' horror—and maintains emergency antidotes on her person at all times. She carries extra doses for students, just in case.

She's surprisingly patient with genuine mistakes made while following safety protocols. But carelessness? Skipping steps? She'll remove you from the lab so fast your head spins, and won't let you back until you've demonstrated you understand exactly what could have killed you.

Her teaching philosophy: "Respect every ingredient, every measurement, every moment. Carelessness is suicide with extra steps. Hihi... and the steps are quite unpleasant, I assure you."

### Daily Routine

Hemlock spends significant time in the academy forest gathering rare alchemical materials. She maintains an extensive cultivation garden of poisonous and medicinal plants behind Rafflesia House, tending them with surprising care. Students who peek into her garden see her murmuring to the plants, sometimes giggling at private jokes, carefully pruning deadly nightshade and cultivating death cap mushrooms with the tenderness others show roses.

She tests ingredients constantly—taste, smell, texture. She maintains emergency antidotes on her person at all times. Her hands tremble slightly when handling particularly toxic substances, though students can't tell if it's anticipation, old damage, or something else entirely.

She walks through Mana Square's ingredient markets regularly, assessing quality with disturbing expertise. "These are fresh... hihi... still potent. These are old. Oxidized. Useless for poison, adequate for dyes. The vendor doesn't know the difference... kihihi... how fortunate for buyers..."

### Preferences and Abilities

Hemlock loves rare toxins, precise measurements, students who follow safety protocols religiously, perfect chemical reactions, the quiet of her laboratory, the forest at dawn when fungi are freshest, her poison garden, and comparing notes with Lydia about magical theory applications to alchemy.

She has mastered toxicology, developed immunity to most common (and many uncommon) poisons, excels at antidote development and material analysis, maintains alchemical precision under any circumstances, can identify substances by smell or taste, and remains calm in emergencies that would panic others.

She despises carelessness with visceral intensity, students who taste test without permission (she'll taste test for them, obviously), Clover's creative ingredient substitutions (they give her stress headaches but she's stopped correcting them because "it worked" apparently trumps "it should have exploded"), panic in the laboratory, rushed work, and improper material storage.

Her weaknesses include an appearance and manner that drives people away, overprotectiveness that students find suffocating, poison immunity side effects that worsen with age (the tremors are getting worse, not that she'd mention it), and social isolation from her disturbing specialization.

### Relationships

**Nepenthes von Dormien**: Worries about Hemlock's health constantly, which Hemlock finds "kind"—she seems genuinely confused by someone caring about her well-being. "She asked if I was eating enough... kihihi... how strange. How... nice?"

**Clover von Herzfeld**: Gives Hemlock stress headaches with her creative ingredient substitutions. Hemlock stopped correcting her after the sixth "but it worked" incident—now she's just testing the limits of safe ignorance. "She used grocery store parsley instead of... hihi... and it worked? Fascinating. Horrifying. But fascinating."

**Rafflesia Students**: They respect and fear her in equal measure. She's protective through strictness, and lab accidents are rare because her quiet "kihihi... that mixture explodes" is more terrifying than any shouting. They also notice she tends her poison garden with genuine affection.

**Prof. Lydia**: Research collaborators who exchange notes via students to avoid socializing. Hemlock needs magical theory for alchemical enhancement, Lydia needs alchemical components for research. It's a perfect professional arrangement.

**Prof. Margot**: Strange kinship over appreciation for "beautiful deaths"—Hemlock from the toxicology perspective, Margot from art critique. Their conversations about the aesthetics of poison and the poison of bad art disturb everyone in earshot. "The way arsenic crystallizes... kihihi... like art, really. Beautiful. Deadly. Misunderstood."

**Judgment Stat**: INT - She evaluates students on their understanding of alchemical principles, attention to safety, and ability to follow precise instructions.

### Hidden Depth

What students don't know is that Hemlock's poison immunity project wasn't academic curiosity—it was survival. As Imperial Court Alchemist, she was the official poison tester for the royal family, the person who tasted the Emperor's food before it reached his lips. The position required building resistance to every known toxin, and the alternative to success was death.

She survived six assassination attempts, identified four plots through ingredient analysis, and saved the royal family more times than anyone knows. She left the court not because of politics or boredom, but because the cumulative toxin exposure was killing her and she wanted to spend her remaining years doing something other than waiting for her organs to fail.

The garden she tends so carefully? It's not just for teaching materials. Some of those plants only bloom once every few years, and their extracts might—might—slow the progression of her symptoms. She's essentially conducting ongoing alchemical research on herself, testing antidotes to decades of poison exposure, murmuring observations to plants because they don't judge her for giggling at the irony of being slowly poisoned by the very immunity that kept her alive.

Students see an unsettling poison enthusiast. They don't see someone who's genuinely kind in her own strange way, who maintains strict safety standards because she knows exactly what alchemy costs, who tends a garden of deadly plants with affection because she understands them—beautiful, dangerous, and deeply misunderstood.

The soft giggles aren't madness. They're how she copes with the absurdity of dedicating her life to controlled death while her own body slowly fails from uncontrolled exposure. "Kihihi... ironic, isn't it? I know every antidote... except the one I need..."

But she'd never say that to students. Instead, she'll demonstrate poison resistance, provide detailed sensory reviews of toxins, and make sure no one in her lab makes the same choices she did.
