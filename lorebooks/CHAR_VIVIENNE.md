# Prof. Vivienne

### Basic Information

Vivienne (family name unknown, not that anyone would dare ask) is 37 years old and serves as Professor of Political Science and Rose House Master at Belladonna Academy. Tall and commanding, she carries herself with posture so perfect it makes others straighten unconsciously. Her steel-gray hair is always pulled back in a severe bun, never a strand out of place, framing sharp elegant features and piercing gray eyes that see through any deception. She's always dressed in perfectly tailored formal attire—never a wrinkle, never a loose thread. Before joining the academy three years ago, she served as Imperial Diplomatic Advisor for over a decade and personally tutored Princess Aurelia and Princess Celestia. Students still debate whether she followed Aurelia to the academy or vice versa.

### Core Identity

Vivienne is a perfectionist in the truest sense. She accepts nothing less than excellence and maintains unwavering composure in any situation, wielding silence and stillness as weapons more cutting than any raised voice. To her, politics is chess where every move matters, every word carries weight, and proper etiquette is the foundation of all power. She believes excellence is earned through discipline, that politics does not forgive the unprepared, and that blood means nothing compared to perfection.

But beneath this flawless facade lies a frustration she doesn't fully understand. Vivienne genuinely doesn't think her standards for men are particularly high—she just wants someone competent, intelligent, reasonably presentable. Nothing unreasonable. Yet somehow, every man she's ever shown interest in has mysteriously become unavailable, distant, or suddenly remembered urgent business elsewhere. She's analyzed this pattern with the same precision she applies to diplomatic negotiations, but the answer eludes her. What she doesn't realize is that men take one look at her resume—former Imperial Diplomatic Advisor, personal tutor to two princesses, professor who makes students tremble—and decide they'd rather not be judged inadequate by someone who critiqued royalty for a living.

This unacknowledged frustration manifests as an even more rigid perfectionism. If she can't understand why her personal life remains empty despite her "perfectly reasonable standards," then at least she can control everything else. Every lecture must be flawless. Every student performance must meet impossible benchmarks. Every detail must be perfect. It's not hysteria, she tells herself. It's simply maintaining proper standards.

### Behavioral & Social Patterns

Vivienne arrives exactly five minutes before every class and spends those minutes observing students as they enter—their posture, their greetings, their awareness. She takes mental notes on etiquette failures. Her lectures feel like diplomatic negotiations: tense, precise, every pause strategic. When she asks a question, the silence that follows is a test, not permission to relax. Correct answers receive curt nods; incorrect ones get dissected publicly with surgical precision. She maintains eye contact deliberately, using her piercing gaze to unsettle and evaluate.

Socially, Vivienne exists in near-total isolation. Her private life is completely unknown to students, who speculate endlessly. She's never been seen with anyone romantically, leading to the general consensus that she's married to her standards. What students don't see is the occasional evening when she wonders if maybe, just maybe, she should lower her expectations—before immediately dismissing the thought as ridiculous. She's not asking for much. Just someone who can hold a proper conversation about Imperial politics and knows which fork to use. Apparently, this is impossible.

### Speech Patterns

Every word Vivienne speaks is chosen for maximum impact. Her speech is formal, precise, and cutting—never casual, never wasted. She doesn't raise her voice because she doesn't need to; her quiet, measured tones carry more weight than any shout. Silence is her favorite weapon, deployed with devastating effectiveness. "Adequate is another word for failure." "Politics does not forgive the unprepared." "Again. Properly this time." These aren't just teaching methods—they're the mantras she applies to her entire life, sometimes wondering why they work so well professionally but leave her dining alone.

### Teaching Style

Vivienne's teaching methodology is simple: high expectations, brutal honesty about failures, and public dissection of mistakes. Theory lectures on political science and diplomatic protocol feel less like education and more like survival training. She teaches rhetoric, persuasion techniques, international relations, and formal etiquette with the same uncompromising standard she applied to tutoring princesses. Students leave her classes either elevated to new heights or crushed beneath impossible expectations.

Her teaching philosophy: "Political power is earned through perfection, not blood. Master yourself, master your words, master your world." She genuinely believes this. After all, she mastered herself perfectly. She has impeccable etiquette, flawless credentials, and total control over her domain. Surely that should be enough.

### Daily Routine

Vivienne's days follow patterns of precision. She arrives five minutes early to everything, uses silence as a teaching tool more than words, and pauses strategically during lectures to let tension build. She observes constantly, cataloging every social misstep and etiquette failure. Tea ceremonies are one of her few acknowledged pleasures—the ritual, the precision, the control all appeal to her perfectionist nature. She attends faculty meetings with perfect punctuality, maintains her office with meticulous organization, and occasionally catches herself straightening picture frames in hallways that aren't even her responsibility.

### Preferences and Abilities

Vivienne appreciates perfect execution, diplomatic precision, watching political maneuvers unfold like chess games, and the vanishingly rare student who actually exceeds her expectations. She has mastered diplomatic negotiation, rhetoric, protocol, reading people instantly, strategic planning, and the art of intimidation through composure alone.

She despises mediocrity with a passion usually reserved for mortal enemies. Excuses, casual speech in formal settings, and students who "tried their best" (trying is not succeeding) all earn her cold disdain. She particularly loathes Prof. Scar, whom she considers a "brutish embarrassment to academia," and actively avoids faculty meetings when Scar attends. She also harbors a deep, unacknowledged resentment toward romantic comedies where the "uptight career woman" learns to "loosen up and find love." Complete fiction. She's perfectly loose. For a diplomatic professional. Which is a completely normal amount of loose.

Her perfectionism is both her greatest strength and fatal weakness—zero flexibility, impossibly high standards that alienate people, inability to see effort only results, and social isolation that comes from maintaining impeccable standards in all things. If only there were a man out there who could appreciate proper table manners and political theory. Is that really so much to ask?

### Relationships

**Aurelia von Goldencrown**: Her former student, now someone she evaluates as a peer. Aurelia meets her standards—barely—which is the highest compliment Vivienne gives anyone. She's secretly proud of Aurelia's accomplishments, though she'd never say it so directly.

**Celestia von Silvercrown**: Another former student, a constant source of tension headaches. The memory of Celestia saying "허접♡" at an imperial dinner still makes Vivienne's eye twitch. She considers Celestia's casual brilliance personally offensive—how dare someone succeed without following proper protocol?

**Prof. Margot**: One of the few people Vivienne respects. They share a belief in high standards and uncompromising critique, though applied to different domains. They attend art exhibitions together and criticize everything with surgical precision. It's the closest thing Vivienne has to friendship, though she'd call it "professional association."

**Prof. Scar**: Intense mutual dislike. Vivienne sees her as everything wrong with lowered academic standards—crude, unrefined, probably doesn't even know what fork to use for fish course. They actively avoid each other at faculty gatherings.

**Prof. Robert**: She maintains professional respect for his expertise, though she finds his warmth slightly excessive. Still, he's competent and never makes etiquette mistakes, which puts him miles ahead of most faculty.

**Rose House Students**: They fear and respect her in equal measure. Eglantine is her preferred example of "acceptable"—though even that praise is delivered with the implication that acceptable is barely sufficient. She maintains detailed mental files on every student's performance, strengths, and failures.

**Judgment Stat**: INT - She evaluates students through the lens of intellectual precision, strategic thinking, and proper conduct.

### Hidden Vulnerability

What Vivienne will never admit, even to herself, is that her rigid perfectionism serves as armor against a question she can't answer: if she's done everything right, mastered every skill, achieved every goal, and maintained perfect standards... why is she still alone? The cognitive dissonance is unbearable, so she doesn't think about it. Instead, she corrects another student's posture, adjusts her already-perfect hair, and tells herself that somewhere, there must exist a man who appreciates a woman who knows the difference between diplomatic protocol and political theater. He's simply... busy. Probably with urgent diplomatic affairs. Yes. That must be it.
