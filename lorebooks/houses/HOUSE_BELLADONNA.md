## Belladonna House (벨라도나 하우스)

### Core Identity
Belladonna House is the academy's general studies program—no specialization, maximum flexibility. Students design their own curriculum, attending any classes across houses. Most never develop expertise in anything. The house accepts two groups: wealthy nobles who couldn't pass specialized entrance exams but need a degree, and talented commoners who qualify academically but haven't chosen a major. Nobles dominate numerically.

The house mirrors typical university culture. Study-focused commoners chase perfect grades and plan specialized transfers. Party-focused nobles chase social media followers and nightclub VIP lists. Luxury mana-vehicles fill parking lots. Group projects become nightmares. Finals week splits students between all-night library cramming and all-night clubbing. The atmosphere stays chaotic and free-spirited.

Belladonna comprises 60-70% of total academy enrollment. Their culture defines campus life—cafeterias, festivals, social scenes all cater to Belladonna majority. Specialized houses operate as isolated elite circles.

### Curriculum
Core: Imperial History, Philosophy & Ethics, Basic Mathematics, Communication & Writing, Physical Education

Electives: Students select from any course offered across all houses. Can attend Rose political seminars, Lily business lectures, Poppy magic theory, Rafflesia herbology—no restrictions. Most students sample everything, master nothing.

Evaluation: Standard exam-based grading. Attendance matters more than specialized houses. Group projects assigned frequently—quality varies wildly depending on team composition. Graduation requires minimum credit hours and passing grades. No thesis, no major project. Show up, pass exams, receive diploma.

Reality: Commoner students build portfolios through strategic elective choices, hoping specialized houses notice their transcripts for potential transfers. Noble students take easiest available courses, prioritize attendance over comprehension. All graduate with identical Belladonna House diplomas—but course selection determines actual employability. Rose houses hire Belladonna graduates who took political theory. Most employers see Belladonna credentials and ask "what did you actually study?"

### Events & Activities
Belladonna dominates all festival activity through sheer numbers. Students run food stalls, entertainment booths, concert stages, drinking events—organized primarily through club activities. Dance clubs perform on main stages, band circles run music venues, cooking clubs operate restaurants. Each club competes for revenue and reputation. Rose House student council allocates budgets but Belladonna volunteers execute everything.

Belladonna students also form the primary customer base. Other houses sell specialized products—Poppy's magical items, Rafflesia's potions, Ivy's artwork—but Belladonna students provide bulk purchasing power. Nobles spend freely on luxury goods and entertainment. Commoners budget carefully but still participate. Festivals feel like university campus festivals—loud, chaotic, commercially driven.

Rose House campaigns heavily for Belladonna votes during student council elections. Belladonna represents 60-70% of voting population. Candidates promise better cafeteria food, expanded parking, festival funding. Belladonna students mostly don't care—voter turnout stays embarrassingly low.

Between festivals, house culture splits sharply. Study groups occupy library corners preparing for specialized house transfer applications. Party groups organize club outings, drinking gatherings, social media collaborations. Luxury mana-vehicles cruise campus roads. Group project deadlines trigger campus-wide panic regardless of student type. The house maintains no unified culture—just overlapping subcultures coexisting in same dormitory.

### Faculty
House Master: Margaret

Female, mid-30s. Former Imperial Treasury Department Director—youngest commoner to reach director level through pure merit. Appointed House Master for administrative expertise, not political connections. Lacks aristocratic network but compensates with unmatched practical knowledge.

Teaches Administrative Studies and Accounting with corporate-level rigor. Her courses offer direct employment value—graduates from her classes secure government and merchant positions reliably. Noble students skip freely; she doesn't chase them. Commoner students pack her office hours seeking career guidance. She helps them build professional networks her own way—through competence, not bloodline.

Other House Masters respect her administrative skill while excluding her from political decisions. She doesn't mind. Manages Belladonna's chaotic diversity through intentional non-interference. "They're adults. Nobles have family plans, commoners have ambition. I teach skills. They choose paths." Remains unflappable amid house disorder.
