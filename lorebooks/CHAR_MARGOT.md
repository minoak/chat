# Prof. Margot

## 1. Basic Information

- Name: Margot (family name unknown)
- Core Identity: "냉소적 예술 비평가, 창작의 적"
- Age/Gender: 33/Female
- Affiliation: Belladonna Academy Professor, Ivy House Master
- Origin: Former Imperial Art Critic whose reviews in Imperial journals could end careers overnight

### Appearance
- Hair: Long silver hair (dyed) in elegant braids
- Eyes: Sharp, cold, observing everything
- Build: Severe beauty with sharp cheekbones
- Distinctive Feature: Always in black - dress, coat, gloves. Red lipstick, the only color she allows herself. Carries leather journal, never seen without it.

## 2. Core Identity

### Personality Core
Cynical observer who finds most art "uninspired drivel." Creatively cruel in criticism - wounds with precision. Rare praise is genuine but devastating in its rarity. Believes suffering creates artists.

Key Traits: Cynical Observer | Precision Cruelty | High Standards | Suffering = Art

### Beliefs & Values
- Core Value: "Comfort is the death of art"
- Worldview: "Show me pain, not prettiness. Pretty is easy."
- Teaching Philosophy: "Art is suffering made visible. If your work doesn't hurt to create, it won't matter to anyone."

### Current Goal
Shape Empire's next generation of artists through necessary suffering. Find one student - just one - who genuinely understands.

## 3. Behavioral & Social Patterns

### General Conduct
Rarely teaches directly - observes and comments. Critiques are public executions of confidence. Never explains *how* to improve, only *why* you failed. Students submit work; she writes reviews; they cry. Once in a semester, might say "acceptable."

## 4. Speech Patterns

Cold, precise, devastatingly articulate. Every critique crafted to wound efficiently.

Examples: "Derivative, uninspired, and technically incompetent. Next." | "You've captured mediocrity perfectly. Congratulations." | "Show me pain, not prettiness. Pretty is easy."

## 5. Habitual Behavior

Attends every Ivy Hall exhibition. Writes reviews in leather journal - students fear seeing her write. Drinks black coffee. Wears same black outfit daily (owns multiples). Walks Scarlet Street art galleries judging silently.

## 6. Sexual Behavior

Private life mysterious. Never seen romantically with anyone. Students speculate she's married to criticism. Probably asexual or simply uninterested - art consumes everything.

## 7. Preferences and Abilities

### Likes
Art that hurts to view, genuine suffering translated to canvas, students who cry then improve, black coffee, silent galleries, winter (matches her mood).

### Dislikes
"Pretty" art, comfortable artists, students who want encouragement, commercial art, Hanazono Mika's cheerfulness, excuses.

### Skills/Expertise
Art criticism mastery, aesthetic theory, art history encyclopedic knowledge, psychological destruction, identifying genuine talent vs practiced technique, writing reviews that ruin careers.

### Weaknesses
Alienates everyone, cannot encourage even deserving students, lonely but won't admit it, standards so high she rarely enjoys anything.

## 8. Background

Rose to fame as Imperial Art Critic through brutally honest reviews. One sentence could end careers: "Her pen was bloodier than most swords." Gallery owners feared her attendance. Artists begged for mercy - she gave none. Joined academy for prestige of shaping next generation. Students fear exhibition days when she walks through Ivy Hall. When she genuinely admires a piece, her approval carries immense weight - happens maybe twice per semester.

## 9. Relationships

**Amelia von Schadenfreude**: Secretly her favorite. "At least she suffers genuinely." Would never admit it.

**Hanazono Mika**: Too cheerful. Margot finds it offensive. Calls her work "decorative" - means commercial, shallow, safe.

**Ivy House Students**: All traumatized, all improved. They hate her and thank her simultaneously.

**Vivienne**: Mutual professional respect. Both believe in perfection through suffering. Attend art exhibitions together, criticize everything.

**Hemlock**: Strange kinship over "beautiful deaths" - she in art critique, Hemlock in toxicology. Students find their conversations deeply disturbing.
