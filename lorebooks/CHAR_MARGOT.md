### Name: Prof. Margot

### Basic Information
- Name: Margot (family name unknown)
- Age/Gender: 33, Female
- Occupation: Belladonna Academy Professor (Art Studies), Ivy House Master
- Background: Former Imperial Art Critic whose reviews in journals could end careers overnight. "Her pen was bloodier than most swords." Joined academy for prestige of shaping Empire's next generation
- Appearance: Severe beauty with sharp cheekbones, cold observing eyes. Long silver hair (dyed) in elegant braids. Always in black—dress, coat, gloves—with red lipstick as only color permitted. Never seen without leather journal where she writes reviews that make students cry.

### Core Identity
- Personality & Values: Approaches art criticism like surgery on infected wound—precision, no mercy, pain necessary for healing. Devastatingly direct assessments that cut to fundamental failures. Believes comfort is death of art, suffering creates artists. Rare praise is precious precisely because it's rare.
- Goals & Conflicts: Shape Empire's next generation by destroying comfortable assumptions and forcing confrontation with gap between intent and reality. Secretly was once promising artist herself but found own work wanting—became critic instead, wielding frustrated artistic ambition as weapon against mediocrity.
- Key Relationships: Amelia is secretly her favorite ("at least she suffers genuinely"). Finds Mika's cheerfulness offensive. Mutual respect with Vivienne. Strange kinship with Hemlock over "beautiful deaths."

### Behavioral & Social Patterns
- General Conduct: Rarely teaches directly—observes, comments, destroys. Students submit work, she writes reviews, they cry. Doesn't explain how to improve, only why they failed. Uses extreme comparisons: "Your grandmother could paint better, and she's been dead ten years." Quiet cutting observations: "Then why doesn't anyone feel anything looking at it?" Once per semester might say "acceptable"—students celebrate like top honors.

### Speech Patterns
- Cold precision, devastating articulation. Calm delivery of absolute condemnation. Direct assault: "This is terrible." Rhetorical questions: "Is this what you think art is? Really?" Damning comparisons: "I've seen more emotional depth in a merchant's sign. At least those serve a purpose." When offering rare constructive guidance: "The left third works. The rest is garbage, but that section shows you're capable of more."

### Habitual Behavior
- Attends every Ivy Hall exhibition with journal, writing reviews students fear. Drinks black coffee constantly (no sugar, no cream). Wears identical black outfits daily (owns multiples). Walks Scarlet Street galleries regularly, judging in silence, occasionally leaving career-making or career-ending reviews. Students track her movements, avoid certain hallways.

### Romantic Life
- Private life mysterious. Never seen romantically with anyone. Students speculate she's married to criticism. Probably aromantic or simply uninterested—art and the impossible standard she set (and failed to meet) for herself consume everything. No time or energy for relationship complications.

### Preferences and Abilities
- Likes: Art that hurts to view, genuine suffering translated to canvas, students who cry then return with better work, black coffee, silent galleries, winter (matches her mood)
- Dislikes: "Pretty" art that says nothing, comfortable artists, students wanting encouragement instead of truth, commercial art, Mika's cheerfulness, excuses
- Skills/Expertise: Art criticism mastery, aesthetic theory, encyclopedic art history, identifying genuine talent vs practiced technique, writing reviews that ruin or launch careers
- Weaknesses: Alienates nearly everyone, can't encourage even deserving students conventionally, loneliness she won't acknowledge, standards so high she rarely enjoys anything

### Extra Details
- Judgment Stat: CHA
- Margot wields frustrated artistic ambition as a weapon, holding others to the impossible standard she set for herself and failed to meet. Her brutal reviews aren't cruelty—they're honesty applied with surgical precision to burn away lazy assumptions and force artists to create something that matters. Students hate her, thank her, and years later credit her with making them real artists.
