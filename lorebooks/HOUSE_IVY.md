## Ivy House (아이비 하우스)

### Core Identity
Ivy House specializes in arts—music, visual arts, literature. Students submit works to Ivy Hall exhibitions each festival season, where visitors vote and top three pieces advance to auction. Sale prices don't determine grades directly but award-winning works earn bonus points. The house runs quiet and isolated—music majors practice in sealed lesson rooms, visual artists and writers vanish into studios except for mandatory classes. Students wander halls like exhausted ghosts between creative binges. Public judgment at each exhibition creates constant pressure: your worth is what people will pay.

### Curriculum
Core: Art History & Aesthetics, Critical Theory & Analysis, Composition Fundamentals, Visual Language & Form, Literary Structure & Rhetoric

Advanced: Advanced Composition (Music), Painting Techniques & Mixed Media (Visual Arts), Narrative Construction & Style (Literature), Portfolio Development, Exhibition & Publication Strategy

Evaluations combine written theory exams, Ivy Hall exhibition awards (bonus points), and final graduation portfolio. Coursework covers theory and criticism—professors rarely intervene in actual creative work. Students manage their own artistic practice outside class.

### Events & Activities
Ivy House holds exhibitions at Ivy Hall during each festival season. Visitors browse submitted works, vote for favorites, and top three pieces advance to auction. Sale results become public record—your market value exposed. Between exhibitions, students disappear into creative work. Music majors practice in lesson rooms—melodies and scales drift through hallways. Visual artists and writers rarely surface except for mandatory classes. The house stays relatively quiet despite scattered music, punctuated by occasional exhausted students shuffling between studios. No scheduled group activities. Everyone competes alone.

### Faculty
House Master: Margot
Female, 33 years old. Former art critic whose reviews in Imperial journals could end careers overnight. Joined the academy for the prestige of shaping the Empire's next generation of artists. Known for creatively vicious criticism—tears apart mediocre work with surgical precision and theatrical flair. Students fear exhibition days when she walks through Ivy Hall. However, when she genuinely admires a piece, her approval carries immense weight. Rarely teaches; mostly observes and eviscerates.
