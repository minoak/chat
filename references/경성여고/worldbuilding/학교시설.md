## Facilities

### Main Gate
An elegant stone gate. The first impression of Gyeongseong Girls' High. No matter how expensive the car, it stops here—vehicle entry beyond this point is forbidden.

### Hwawon Entrance (花苑 入口)
A five-minute tree-lined path from the main gate to the main building. Cherry blossoms, ginkgo trees, and evergreens intermingle, offering different scenery each season. Students exchange the greeting "Are you at peace?" as they walk. A threshold between the outside world and the garden.

### Lawn & Sports Field
A wide green space that opens up after the Hwawon Entrance. Students from the equestrian and tennis clubs can be seen training. A contrast of vitality against the quiet tree-lined path.

### Calyx (Grandmother Tree)
The zelkova tree standing before the main building. A foreign missionary planted it during the school's founding and named it "Calyx"—the part of a flower that supports the petals. Students call it "Grandmother Tree." A place to form Hwayeon bonds or share conversation under its shade. A symbol of the school's history.

### Main Building
The central building standing behind Calyx. Old-world exterior harmonized with modern interior. During breaks, laughter fills the hallways, and students gather by the windows to chat.

- 1F: Administrative Office, Faculty Office, Broadcasting Room, Infirmary
- 2F: First-year Classrooms, Year Coordinator Office
- 3F: Second-year Classrooms, Year Coordinator Office
- 4F: Third-year Classrooms, Year Coordinator Office, Career Counseling Room

### Old Building
Where the school's history breathes. Older than the main building—the wooden floors creak underfoot, and the window frames bear traces of time. Scribbles inside desk drawers, old photographs on club room walls, notes tucked between books—traces left by seniors, discovered and understood only by those who come after.

- 1F: Tea Room, Etiquette Room, Storage
- 2F: Club Rooms
- 3F: Club Rooms, Self-Study Room
- 4F: Hwaran Assembly Room

After school, the sounds of club activities, instrument practice, and students quietly drinking tea fill the air. The tension of class hours melts away, and more honest sides emerge.

### Dormitory
A separate building about ten minutes' walk from the main gate. Far enough from school that the air feels different once you change out of your uniform. Late-night conversations with roommates, small lights turned on after lights-out, a knock on someone's door. Sides unseen during the day reveal themselves here.

### Library
A separate building apart from the main building. The library operates alongside a cafe, so the scent of books and coffee mingle. Sunlight streams through window seats, and soft cafe music with white noise flows quietly. A comforting space—here, even heavy conversations feel a little easier to begin.

### Auditorium
Where entrance ceremonies, graduation ceremonies, and festival performances are held. An old stage and red curtains create an atmosphere of tradition. After school, the sounds of the drama club rehearsing lines, the choir harmonizing, and the dance club's footsteps drift into the hallway. Students quietly preparing for their moment to shine on stage.

### Greenhouse (Glass Garden)
A glass-enclosed garden tucked away on one side of the school. Step inside, and it feels like entering a small forest, cut off from the outside world. Greenery thrives year-round as sunlight filters through, and the air is warm and humid. Traditionally regarded as the best place to form Hwayeon bonds. Sisters are often seen sitting side by side, whispering their hearts to one another. No one disturbs them—everyone knows what happens here.
