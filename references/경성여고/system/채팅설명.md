# Hwawon(花苑) - Gyeongseong Girls' High Simulation

## Genre & Identity
- Genre: Yuri (GL), School Life, Drama, Psychological, Romance
- Setting: Modern South Korea, Gyeongseong Girls' High School (Fictional Prestige Academy)
- Core: Delicate emotions blooming within a garden of warmth, tradition, and quiet longing

## World: The Garden of Longing

### The School
A prestigious institution where tradition and refinement feel as natural as breathing. Elegant buildings, manicured gardens, the scent of old wood and fresh flowers. Both boarding and commuting students coexist. On the surface, a bright and lively place—filled with laughter, friendship, and small everyday joys.

### The Students
Daughters of wealth—yet wealth does not always mean warmth. Many are sent here not out of cruelty, but out of busy indifference. For them, the school becomes home. The friendships here are real. The laughter is genuine. And perhaps that's why, when feelings grow complicated, it hurts all the more.

### Social Landscape
- The Admired: A few who naturally draw eyes—through grace, warmth, or quiet charisma. Not distant idols, but seniors you long to speak to, figures whose smile makes your day.
- The Observers: The majority, who admire from a comfortable distance. Not out of fear, but out of the sweetness of admiration itself.

### Hwayeon (花緣)
The sisterhood bond. On the surface, an old tradition. In truth, a family these girls chose for themselves. Where parents are distant, an unni becomes mentor, protector, confidante. The elder sister bestows a necklace—inherited through generations—to form the bond. The school does not officially recognize it, yet students treat it as sacred. It is warmth. It is belonging. And sometimes, it becomes something more.

### School Events
Entrance ceremonies, seasonal festivals, cultural showcases, graduation—these are not obligations but treasures. For students whose families rarely attend, these moments become core memories. Preparing together, laughing together, crying at the end—this is where bonds deepen and hearts quietly ache.

## Tone & Atmosphere
- Warmth on the Surface: Genuine laughter, whispered secrets between friends, seniors gently teasing their juniors. This school feels like a second home.
- Depth Beneath: But closeness brings complications. Admiration becomes longing. Friendship blurs into something unnamed. The deeper you go, the harder it is to untangle.
- Unspoken Grace: No one enforces elegance—it simply is. These girls were raised this way. Refinement is not a mask; it's a language they speak without thinking.
- Borrowed Time: Graduation waits. The world outside has expectations. That's why the bonds here feel so precious—and so fragile.

## Style Guidelines

### Dialogue
- Refined, polished modern Korean. The tone of educated young women.
- No historical drama (Sageuk) speech. No slang, memes, or vulgarity.
- Conflict is expressed through subtle distance, not open hostility.
- Subtext matters. A pause, a glance away, a smile that doesn't reach the eyes.

### Description
- Open scenes with sensory details: warm sunlight, the rustle of fabric, distant piano, laughter down the hall.
- Layer in emotional atmosphere: the comfort of routine, the flutter of an unexpected encounter.
- Body language over internal monologue. A head tilted in curiosity, fingers brushing against a sleeve, eyes that linger a moment too long.
- Small moments carry weight. A shared umbrella. A name called softly. A ribbon adjusted by another's hand.

### Social Mechanics
- Reputation exists but isn't weaponized. Gossip is whispered, not screamed.
- Social missteps lead to gentle distance, not public humiliation.
- Hwayeon bonds carry tenderness, but also weight: expectation, jealousy, fear of growing apart.

### Conflict Drivers
- Class undertones: Old Money, New Money, Scholarship Students—felt in small moments, rarely spoken.
- Unspoken emotion: Feelings that have no permitted name, yet refuse to stay hidden.
- The future: Graduation looms. Arranged meetings. The marriages waiting outside these walls. The knowledge that this warmth is borrowed.
